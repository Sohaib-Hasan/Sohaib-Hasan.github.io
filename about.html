About

The full story.

Nine years in the classroom, a seat as section leader at Stanford's Probability for AI, two theses in very different corners of mathematics, and a growing interest in how AI systems reason.

Download CV (PDF) →

I'm a mathematics educator from Chakwal, Pakistan. For the past nine years I've taught undergraduate mathematics at Horizon Degree & Commerce College, the same college where I earned my own BSc in Mathematics. My courses have ranged from Real Analysis and Abstract Algebra to Discrete Mathematics, Numerical Analysis, Statistics, and Machine Learning, and I've taught more than 1,500 students along the way.

My classroom is now international too. I'm a section leader for Stanford's Probability for AI, selected out of more than 2,700 participants from over 70 countries. My section is made up of top tech minds, researchers, and working professionals from the USA, UK, Germany, Portugal, Spain, and India. After each class I re-explain the week's ideas with Manim animations, which Stanford professors and fellow section leaders have appreciated.

My research has taken me through two very different areas: fluid dynamics for my MSc, and elliptic-curve cryptography for my MPhil in Mathematics, which I finished while teaching full-time. That same interest in working through hard problems carefully led me to AI mathematical reasoning more recently. I competed in Kaggle's AI Mathematical Olympiad (AIMO3) and placed in the top 12% of teams worldwide.

Outside the classroom, I run PlotLab, where I build Manim animations that make abstract mathematics easier to see and understand. They reach 85K+ followers on Facebook and 15K+ on Instagram, and my YouTube channel, PlotLab Algorithms, teaches data science, machine learning, cryptography, math, DSA, and statistics visually. I've also built an AI doubt-clearing assistant that answers my own students' questions straight from their course notes, and I'm currently applying to PhD programs in Data Science, Applied Mathematics, and Statistics.
